What Age Should My Child Start Piano Lessons?

By Michael Krzankowski · Updated August 2026

This is the question I hear most from parents, and my answer after more than twenty years of teaching hundreds of students is: usually around age seven, but readiness matters more than the number.

I generally take students ages seven and up. That isn't an arbitrary cutoff. Piano asks a lot of small hands and young attention spans at the same time: reading symbols on a page, mapping them to fingers, keeping a steady beat, and sitting with one activity long enough to make progress. Most children grow into that combination somewhere around first or second grade — but every child is different, and I've met eight-year-olds who weren't quite there yet and seven-year-olds who were more than ready.

Signs your child is ready

Rather than watching the calendar, watch for these:

  • They can focus on one activity for 15–20 minutes — a puzzle, a drawing, a book. Lessons involve breaks and variety, but a baseline of focus makes everything easier.
  • They know left from right and can count to ten — both come up constantly at the keyboard.
  • They show interest in music on their own — singing along in the car, tapping rhythms, reaching for the keys when they pass a piano. Curiosity is the best fuel a beginner can have.
  • Their fine motor skills are coming along — holding a pencil comfortably, using scissors, buttoning buttons. Piano refines these skills further, but it helps to have a foundation.

What if my child is younger than seven?

Don't rush it. Starting too early can turn the piano into a source of frustration before it ever becomes a source of joy. In the meantime, the best preparation is simply a musical home: sing together, play music you love, dance in the kitchen, and if you have a keyboard, let them explore it freely without any pressure to "practice." A child who arrives at their first lesson already thinking of music as fun is miles ahead of one who arrives thinking of it as a chore.

Is it ever too late to start?

Absolutely not — and I mean that literally. I teach beginners of every age: children, teenagers, and adults, including students who touched a piano for the first time in their forties and fifties. Adults often progress faster than children at first because they understand the why behind what they're practicing. If you've always wanted to play, the best age to start is the age you are now.

How lessons work for young beginners

Every student I teach gets a custom lesson plan built around their age, attention span, and interests. For young beginners that means short, varied activities, plenty of encouragement, and music they actually recognize and enjoy. Lessons happen in your home, on your instrument, which for young children removes the friction of a new environment — they learn where they'll practice.

And twice a year, students of every level perform at our recitals. For a seven-year-old, walking up and playing their piece for a room of cheering families is a confidence-builder that spills into everything else they do.

The bottom line

Around age seven, when the readiness signs are there, is the sweet spot for most children — and there's no upper limit at all.
